# Phase 9A — Bybit 24-month external QML replication

The pre-result amendment replaced the longest-common-sample rule with the latest complete common 24-month interval because streaming transaction-level futures flow over the longer history was computationally costly. It was hashed before panel construction and before any forecast result was generated or viewed. The frozen Bybit window is 2024-10-06 20:00 through 2026-10-06 20:00 UTC, with 4,381 four-hour forecast origins per asset. The common chronological cutoff is 2026-03-01 20:00 UTC. After the six-origin horizon purge, BTC and ETH each use 2,880 training dates and the same 1,315 final holdout dates, from 2026-03-01 20:00 through 2026-10-06 20:00 UTC.

Only Bybit passed the venue data-quality gate. OKX was excluded before forecasting because the recorded historical open-interest probes returned no records. All seven required Bybit/Binance semantic-parity checks passed. All 2,924 required transaction archives were parsed to end-of-file, schema/timestamp checked, and SHA-256 verified before and after model execution. The 5-minute spot source had no missing or duplicate bars in-window. Required spot, funding, OI-change, basis, futures-flow and target coverage was 100% for both assets. The 180-observation rolling-shock warm-up leaves 4,201 complete common model dates. One OI log-change above 25% was flagged for each asset; no observations were removed on that basis.

The amendment began with 131.76 GB of raw files already present. It downloaded a further 32.45 GB and processed 114.07 GB of transaction archives from inside the frozen interval. It fully read 2,924 files and skipped 2,786 archive files outside the primary interval; those files were retained. The BTC monthly-cache write span was approximately 18.1 minutes by file timestamps, ETH streaming aggregation took 12.7 minutes, and final panel assembly took 8.7 seconds. Process peak RAM was not instrumented. The pre-model freeze passed with SHA-256 `11344198427465e1b28e99694520252b1a7c1dea8d8f08f64ead1f76119dcb68`; both frozen QML fixtures passed. The final holdout was not used to select or tune models.

The target is the frozen arithmetic mean of the next six 4-hour Amihud observations, with `1e6 × |4-hour log return| / USDT quote volume` and the frozen `log1p` transform. On this small numerical scale, transformed and original-scale MAEs are very close. BTC used the frozen Hybrid QNN with four qubits; ETH used the frozen QNN with six qubits. Each was compared with its frozen five-seed matched MLP. No architecture, feature set, transformation, qubit count, seed, period or venue was selected using holdout performance.

For BTC, transformed-target MAE was 1.43840e-5 for SpotOnly, 1.42895e-5 for SpotOnly plus MLP residual correction, and 1.40248e-5 for SpotOnly plus QML correction. Thus the point estimates favor QML: Delta_Q was +2.6475e-7 and Delta_I was +3.5920e-7. Neither difference is supported by paired temporal inference: the 42-bar Delta_Q interval was [-6.1288e-8, 6.2325e-7] (Holm p=0.2637), and the Delta_I interval was [-3.9301e-8, 6.9595e-7] (Holm p=0.0579). The corresponding 84-bar intervals also include zero. Only 3 of 5 seeds favored QML over MLP. BTC therefore fails the frozen replication rule despite the favorable point estimates.

For ETH, transformed-target MAE was 7.05130e-5 for SpotOnly, 7.20959e-5 for SpotOnly plus MLP, and 7.24389e-5 for SpotOnly plus QML. Delta_Q was -3.4301e-7 and Delta_I was -1.9259e-6, so QML underperformed both comparators. The 42-bar Delta_I interval was [-3.5907e-6, -2.9869e-7] with Holm p=0.0300; the 84-bar interval was [-3.5059e-6, -3.0865e-7]. This is evidence that the frozen ETH QML correction worsened the complete forecast on this holdout. Only 1 of 5 seeds favored QML over MLP. Delta_Q did not survive bootstrap/Holm correction.

On the original Amihud scale, MAE for BTC was 1.43848e-5 (SpotOnly), 1.42903e-5 (MLP corrected), and 1.40255e-5 (QML corrected). For ETH it was 7.05298e-5, 7.21130e-5, and 7.24560e-5, respectively. Original-scale RMSE and median absolute error are retained alongside these in `primary_metrics.json`.

The bootstrap results agree across 7-day and 14-day blocks: no positive Delta_Q or Delta_I is established for BTC, while ETH Delta_I is negative under both block lengths. Supporting HAC inference is consistent with the negative ETH Delta_I; BTC HAC is more favorable than its block-bootstrap result, which is a material inferential disagreement. Holm correction was applied across BTC and ETH separately for Delta_Q and Delta_I. No positive primary result survives it.

The matched impossible-time placebo produced no statistically supported positive QML correction. BTC placebo Delta was +1.8896e-7 with a 42-bar interval spanning zero (p=0.144). ETH placebo Delta was negative, -2.8065e-6, with Holm p=0.002; the correction worsened the placebo forecast rather than improving it. The frozen material-positive-placebo warning therefore did not trigger, though the control is not a two-sided null for ETH.

Answers to the study questions:

1. **Frozen BTC Hybrid-QNN-4q versus MLP:** BTC QML has a lower point-estimate MAE, but paired bootstrap/Holm inference does not support the difference and only 3/5 seeds favor QML.
2. **Frozen ETH QNN-6q versus MLP:** QML is worse by point estimate; only 1/5 seeds favor it, with no paired support for QML superiority.
3. **BTC SpotOnly plus QML versus SpotOnly:** the point estimate improves, but both block-bootstrap intervals cross zero and Holm p=0.0579.
4. **ETH SpotOnly plus QML versus SpotOnly:** the full forecast is worse; the negative difference survives Holm correction and both block lengths.
5. **Bootstrap, Holm and seeds:** they do not support a stable positive QML gain. The ETH harm is stable across bootstrap block lengths and Holm-adjusted.
6. **Negative controls:** no positive impossible-time gain is supported; ETH's placebo correction is significantly negative.
7. **Cross-asset and cross-venue replication:** the pattern does not replicate across BTC and ETH. Only Bybit passed the data gate, so cross-venue replication was unavailable.
8. **Incremental derivatives information:** it is not established. BTC is inconclusive and ETH shows a significant adverse QML correction on the holdout.
9. **Algorithmic QML improvement:** no robust QML-over-MLP advantage is established. The BTC point-estimate advantage is not seed-stable or statistically supported.

The frozen script's `paper_level_decision.json` mechanically labels the run `ALGORITHMIC_QML_GAIN_WITHOUT_INCREMENTAL_INFORMATION` because BTC has a positive point Delta_Q while its Delta_I interval crosses zero. That label overstates the inferential evidence: BTC Delta_Q itself has no paired-bootstrap/Holm support and only 3/5 seeds favor QML, while ETH Delta_Q is negative. Neither asset satisfies the frozen PASS rule. The strict paper-level replication outcome is therefore `NO_EXTERNAL_QML_REPLICATION`; the raw script label is preserved and the distinction is recorded in `classification_audit.json` and `paper_level_decision_adjudicated.json`.

The narrow defensible conclusion is that the previously observed 24-hour Amihud signal did not externally replicate under these frozen Bybit QML configurations. The BTC point estimate is favorable but uncertain; the ETH correction degrades forecast accuracy. This does not erase the prior Phase 6 exploratory signal or Phase 7/8 generalization failures. It supports no claim of causal derivatives effects, universal derivatives predictive value, general QML superiority, computational quantum advantage, or hardware quantum advantage. Simulation used exact statevectors only.

The full primary run and network-disabled reproduction matched byte-for-byte for all six forecast Parquet files and the delta, bootstrap, Holm, placebo and decision JSON files. Primary metric fields matched exactly, all frozen inputs and prior-stage hashes remained unchanged, and the post-model raw SHA-256 audit passed for all 2,924 archives.
