# Phase 7 external liquidity replication

The frozen development protocol was applied to cached, same-venue Binance data. The new external evaluation uses feature dates from 2024-03-04 through 2026-09-29 20:00 UTC, with future 24-hour labels ending by 2026-09-30 20:00 UTC. The model-specific complete-case samples begin on 2024-03-05 04:00 UTC because of the frozen lagged-feature and availability rules; there are 5,480–5,526 scored feature dates per asset/target comparison. The output contains 2,149,446 prediction rows across five assets, three distinct liquidity targets, legacy/enhanced comparisons and six learned model families.

The confirmatory Amihud comparison does not meet the frozen replication criteria. In the legacy feature specification, the equal-category paired transformed-MAE difference (Spot+Derivatives minus Spot-only; negative favors added derivatives) is -0.00443415 for BTC (42-interval block-bootstrap 95% CI -0.00673856 to -0.00214898) and -0.00193601 for ETH (CI -0.0040808 to 0.000361241). Only 3 of five eligible assets improve; BTC has 2/5 and ETH 3/5 model categories improving. The multi-condition primary rule therefore fails even though BTC's aggregate interval is below zero.

The result is model-sensitive. For Amihud in BTC, the six individual model families split 2/6 in favor of added derivatives and only 1/6 both improve and survive the within-comparison Holm adjustment. ETH splits 3/6, with 2/6 Holm-surviving improvements. Large neural-family gains coexist with deterioration in several other families, so a best-model result would overstate robustness.

The two distinct liquidity proxies do not generalize the BTC/ETH result. For Kyle price impact, BTC's legacy equal-category delta is -0.00109791 and ETH's is 0.00562137. For Corwin-Schultz, the corresponding deltas are 0.00223154 and 0.00233915. Signs and uncertainty do not satisfy the frozen two-asset and category-consistency rule for either proxy. Thus the evidence is not general across liquidity measurement.

Fixed external subperiods also show no stable BTC/ETH pattern. BTC 2024-03_to_2024-12: delta -0.00568035; Spot-only MAE 0.182108; relative change -3.12%. BTC 2025: delta 0.00180065; Spot-only MAE 0.204607; relative change 0.88%. BTC 2026_partial: delta -0.0113152; Spot-only MAE 0.222118; relative change -5.09%. ETH 2024-03_to_2024-12: delta -0.00446373; Spot-only MAE 0.149731; relative change -2.98%. ETH 2025: delta 0.00147505; Spot-only MAE 0.162299; relative change 0.91%. ETH 2026_partial: delta -0.00371413; Spot-only MAE 0.180613; relative change -2.06%. The complete segment details for both assets are in `phase7_replication_decision.json`; the predeclared requirement is not met if fewer than two of three segments improve for either asset or any deterioration exceeds 5% of Spot-only segment MAE.

Amihud stress-warning metrics are mixed across assets. Equal-category average F1 changes from 0.189 to 0.207 for BTC and from 0.270 to 0.346 for ETH, while BNB moves from 0.240 to 0.120. For SOL, no external observations crossed the development q90 event threshold, so its precision/recall/lift are not informative. These are fixed-threshold descriptive warnings, not evidence of causal transmission.

The impossible-time diagnostics ran with fixed Ridge settings, but the stored diagnostics contain their raw MAE only and no matched Spot-only benchmark. They are therefore insufficient to establish whether post-outcome derivative information adds predictive value. This limitation is explicitly retained rather than calling the negative controls a pass.

The defensible conclusion is that the Stage-6 BTC/ETH Amihud forecasting result is **not externally replicated under the frozen multi-condition rule**, and the apparent added predictive value is sensitive to asset, model family, feature specification and liquidity proxy. This is predictive evidence only; it does not identify a causal effect. The three liquidity measures capture different concepts: Amihud price impact per turnover, Kyle signed-flow price impact and a Corwin-Schultz range-based spread proxy.

The first external scoring attempt completed all forecast predictions but stopped in its final diagnostic because `StandardScaler` was missing from the negative-control routine. Its incomplete prediction file was archived and not inspected for performance. The fixed code was rerun without changing any protocol/model choice; the final prediction artifact has the same SHA-256 as that incomplete attempt, demonstrating byte-identical forecast reproduction. Thus the frozen external forecast pipeline was executed twice for engineering recovery, with no interim result inspection or result-guided choice; the first run produced no complete report. The evaluation summary and negative-control outputs were then completed successfully.
