# Phase 6 research summary

**Benchmark status:** completed once on the frozen final test after development-only tuning. The target is predictive performance, not causal identification. The source panel, protocol, and selected-model hashes match their frozen values.

The benchmark used the certified 4-hour BTC and ETH panel. Development covered 2021-12-01 through 2023-06-30 20:00 UTC; the final feature period began 2023-07-01 00:00 UTC. Each asset was modeled separately. Targets were future realized volatility, future downside semivariance, and future average Amihud over 4, 8, and 24 hours. Inputs were six intervals of frozen spot history, with either no derivatives or all four certified derivatives (funding, OI log change, futures taker imbalance, and basis). All comparisons used identical complete-case rows; target windows began after the feature interval.

Continuous forecasts were scored primarily by log1p-target MAE, with RMSE, original-scale errors, and QLIKE for volatility retained as secondary metrics. The final usable test counts were 1,422, 1,421, and 1,417 rows per asset and target at 4-, 8-, and 24-hour horizons. The final target windows ended no later than 2024-03-03 20:00 UTC.

Development tuning used three expanding temporal folds, with fixed bounded grids. Benchmarks included persistence and training-mean baselines, OLS, Ridge, RBF-SVR, ExtraTrees, HistGradientBoosting, a compact MLP, a compact GRU, and a fixed equal-weight ensemble. The ensemble had no fitted stacker. Larger sequence models, extra boosting packages, and AutoML were excluded because they were unnecessary for the modest sample and would expand the search. Continuous targets made class imbalance and classification calibration inapplicable.

Under the frozen task rule, one task replicated across BTC and ETH: 24-hour future Amihud. BTC’s category-average relative log-MAE reduction was 18.56% (paired block-bootstrap 95% interval 10.98% to 30.15%); ETH’s was 13.64% (5.78% to 23.13%). Each passed the preregistered operational task rule. No realized-volatility or downside-risk task passed it, and Amihud at 4 and 8 hours did not pass. Thus the pattern is narrow and horizon-specific rather than broad across spot conditions.

The result requires a substantial qualification. In both 24-hour Amihud tasks, Holm-supported positive categories were the neural category and the equal-weight ensemble; the ensemble itself contains the neural models, so these are not independent forecast constructions in a strict statistical sense. On the same tasks, linear and tree gains were small or mixed; BTC’s linear category significantly deteriorated. Across all 18 tasks, average relative log-MAE reductions were negative for OLS/Ridge, kernel, bagged trees, boosting, neural models, and the ensemble. The neural forecasts were also far worse in absolute error than simple or tree baselines for tiny Amihud targets. Therefore the frozen algorithmic label is target-specific predictive value, but the evidence is model-sensitive and should not be described as broadly robust or practically established.

Component ablations did not identify a stable driver. Across all task/model comparisons, the average relative changes versus Spot Only were negative for adding basis, funding, OI, or futures flow. The 24-hour Amihud component patterns were asset-dependent. These ablations are predictive comparisons only and do not identify a causal contribution.

**Frozen benchmark classification:** `C_DERIVATIVES_PROVIDE_TARGET_SPECIFIC_PREDICTIVE_VALUE`, because the registered task rule passed for the same target and horizon in BTC and ETH. The classification is restricted to the single 24-hour Amihud task and is qualified by overlapping neural/ensemble evidence and weak absolute neural accuracy.

The Stage-3 daily result remains INCONCLUSIVE and is not overturned: this benchmark finds a narrow conditional out-of-sample prediction pattern at 4-hour sampling, not a stable directional hierarchy. It does not establish that derivatives cause spot-market changes, that the result generalizes beyond Binance BTC/ETH or this holdout period, or that any one derivative component is responsible. The strongest defensible statement is that the full derivatives information set showed a protocol-qualified, target-specific improvement for 24-hour Amihud forecasts in both assets, but the improvement is concentrated in neural/ensemble forecasts and is not broad across model classes or risk targets.
